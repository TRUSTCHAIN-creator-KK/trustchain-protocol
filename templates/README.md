# Plain-words agreement templates

Eight short templates for everyday deals between people: a repair job, a freelance project, a loan between friends, a car sale. They are written in plain words, so both people can read them and know what they agreed.

Each template covers the five things most small-deal arguments come back to:

1. **What we're doing.** What is included, and what is not.
2. **Price and payment.** Amounts as numbers. When, and how.
3. **Changes.** How a change is asked for, priced and agreed, in writing.
4. **Dates.** Start, finish, and what moves a date.
5. **If someone walks.** What is owed if it stops early, in either direction.

## How each template is laid out

- **Short form.** One text message, three or four sentences, ending with "Reply yes". For many small deals, a text and a yes are enough to remember what was agreed. Send it and keep the reply.
- **Full form.** The two people, the five lines above, and an "Agreed" block.
- **Optional clauses.** One or two sentences each. Add the ones that fit.
- **Example.** A filled-in short form.
- **Check the rules where you live.** What to look up locally before you rely on it.

Fill in every `[BRACKET]`. Where a bracket shows choices split by a slash, keep one. Write money as numbers and dates as dates: "a bit off the rent" and "materials extra" are where arguments start. If something changes later, write the change as a new note. Do not edit the old one.

## What a template can and cannot do

A written agreement records what two people said, so neither has to rely on memory later. It does not decide whether a term is allowed where you live. Rules about rent, interest, deposits, permits and licences differ by place, which is why every template ends with a "check the rules where you live" note.

These templates work on paper, in an email or in a text thread. You can also draft one at [trustchain.biz](https://trustchain.biz).
